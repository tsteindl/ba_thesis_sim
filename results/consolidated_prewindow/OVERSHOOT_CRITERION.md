## 1. Short answer to your question

**No, we are not in the dark, and the criterion is not "empirical only".** It has a clean
mechanism, and the reason it was hard to see is that we were auditing the wrong thing. The
Gaussian was never load-bearing for the criterion. Two exact facts carry it:

**(a) The fold.** Past the first aliasing boundary the estimator folds onto the principal branch —
your own Equation (2.85), `phi_hat = arccos|cos(N phi)|/N`. So overshooting shifts the estimator's
mean *down*, by a relative amount

```
|phi - mean| / phi  =  2 (1 - 1/r),        r = N phi / (pi/2).
```

This is exact and assumes nothing about the distribution. The criterion is a **mean-shift
detector**, and this is the shift it detects.

**(b) The degeneracy is the mechanism, not the problem.** As `r -> 1` the readout probability
`p_0 = cos^2(N phi)` collapses, so `K = 0` with probability `-> 1` and the estimator concentrates on
the single atom `pi/(2N)`. Comparing that atom against a reference near `phi` is *exactly*
comparing `pi/2` against `N phi` — the overshoot condition itself. Below the boundary the atom sits
**above** `phi`; above it, **below**. So the test is sharpest precisely where the Gaussian
approximation is worst. In the left panel of the figure the exact-reference curve is a near-perfect
step at `r = 1`.

This is why the earlier audit felt like a dead end: it kept reporting that normality fails at the
boundary, which is true and *is the point*. The failure has the right sign.

**Where the Gaussian actually earns its place** is the other direction — `z_alpha` in Eq. (3.6),
and Theorem 3.2.1's truncated-normal posterior for the safeguard. Both are evaluated under the
**safe** hypothesis, where Lemma 2.6.3 is accurate (exact KS `<= 0.084`, SD within `2.2 %`, over the
regular region at every selected shot count). That is the division of labour to state in the thesis:

| Claim | Rests on | Verified by |
|---|---|---|
| overshoot criterion detects aliasing | the fold, Eq. (2.85) — exact | Table `tab:overshoot-operating`, `fig_overshoot_criterion` |
| `alpha` sets the false-alarm rate | Lemma 2.6.3, under the *safe* hypothesis | the Section 2.7 Gaussianity table |
| safeguard's posterior | Lemma 2.6.3 + Theorem 3.2.1 | the Section 2.7 Gaussianity table |
| end-to-end overshoot 0.74 % | none of the above alone | Tables 4.5/4.6 |

### The one genuinely weak link

At `alpha = 0.5` — the value your grid search selects — `z_alpha = 0` and the Gaussian **drops out
of the decision entirely**: the rule is just `phi_hat_N < phi_hat_acc`. What the analysis does show
is that the *nominal* `alpha` is not the achieved false-alarm rate once the reference is noisy:

| nominal `alpha` | exact reference | `rho = 3` | `rho = 6` |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.05 | 2.6 % | 28.0 % | 36.0 % |

That is a **quantified version of the caveat your text already makes** ("the uncertainty of this
reference estimate is not included"). It also explains why `alpha` had to be tuned rather than set
to a conventional 0.05: as a confidence level it is not meaningful, as an ROC operating point it is.
Say that, and the heuristic stops looking like a gap and starts looking like a deliberate choice.

algorithm: **the detector sharpens as the search proceeds**, because each accepted probe gives the
next comparison a deeper and more precise reference.

> The rule is a mean-shift detector, and the shift it exploits is exact rather than statistical.
> By \eqref{eq:folded-estimator}, overshooting folds the estimator onto the principal branch and
> lowers its mean by a relative amount $2(1-1/r)$, where $r = N\phi/(\pi/2)$. As $r \to 1$ the
> readout probability $p_0$ collapses, so $K=0$ with probability approaching one and $\hat\phi_N$

> resulting operating characteristic. It also shows that the achieved false-alarm rate matches the
> nominal $\alpha$ only when the reference is exact: because the uncertainty of
> $\hat\phi_{\mathrm{acc}}$ is not propagated, $\alpha$ acts as a tuned operating point rather than
> a confidence level.
